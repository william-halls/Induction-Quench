# Quartz Glass Tube Chamber

Concept for a **compact, low-volume vacuum chamber** to enable faster sample quenching cycles. Based on a sealed quartz tube with integrated top/bottom plugs.

## Design Concept

### Advantages
- ✓ **Compact** — Low air volume means faster vacuum evacuation
- ✓ **Modular** — Coil sits outside the chamber (simpler wiring/plumbing)
- ✓ **Integrated Design** — All feedthroughs in top plug (vacuum, argon, pressure, thermocouple)

### Configuration

**Top Plug** — Housed:
- Vacuum/vent line
- Argon backfill line  
- Pressure gauge monitoring
- Linear actuation rod seal (for drop mechanism)
- Thermocouple feedthrough

**Bottom Section** — Drain spout:
- Diaphragm pump controls water flow
- Allows vacuum pull and argon backfill without water boiling
- Pre-fill with water before quench to reduce humidity

**Optional** — Atmospheric conditioning:
- Fill tube with water pre-quench to remove dissolved humidity (theoretical benefit, likely unnecessary)

## Why Abandoned

### Critical Manufacturing Issues

1. **Quartz Sealing Complexity**
   - Quartz tubes require special fittings with even pressure distribution
   - Prevents cracking when vacuum is applied
   - High cost and precision machining required

2. **Top Plug Design Complexity**
   - Multiple sealed penetrations (5+ feedthroughs) in compact space
   - Achieving simultaneous vacuum-tight seals difficult
   - Failure risk too high for initial prototype

3. **Sample Holder Constraint**
   - Holder must not obstruct argon flow around sample
   - Requires precise mechanical design
   - Takes up valuable volume in narrow tube

## Reconsideration Criteria

Could revisit if:
- Pre-made quartz vacuum systems become available
- Simpler, off-the-shelf quick-disconnect fittings for quartz
- Budget/timeline allows prototype iteration

## Status

🔴 **Abandoned** — Complexity not justified compared to bucket design
